How it works

A gateway, not another provider

Panmail doesn't replace SendGrid, Mailgun or your own Postfix box — it stands in front of all of them. Your application talks to one API. Panmail decides where the mail actually goes, and what happens when that fails.

  1. 01

    Point your app at one endpoint

    Swap your SMTP client for a single authenticated call. Send raw HTML, plain text, or a template ID with variables — attachments included.

  2. 02

    Panmail routes it

    The sending domain picks the provider, so a tenant can never send as a domain that isn't theirs. If the primary refuses, the next one takes it.

  3. 03

    The outbox guarantees it

    Accepted mail lands in a persistent outbox before anything is attempted. Temporary failures retry on your backoff pattern — 5m, 1h, 1d — instead of vanishing.

  4. 04

    You see what happened

    Sent, delivered, opened, clicked, bounced — unified across every provider, streamed to your own systems over signed webhooks.

Deliverability

Your sender reputation is an asset. Panmail treats it like one.

Most outages aren't outages. They're a slow slide into the spam folder because nobody stopped mailing the addresses that keep bouncing.

  • Hard bounces are suppressed immediately. An address that will never accept mail is added to the suppression list and never attempted again — across every tenant that shares the list.
  • Soft bounces retry on your schedule. Mailbox full, greylisted, rate limited — classified as temporary and retried with a per-tenant backoff pattern rather than hammered or discarded.
  • Unsubscribes are honoured at the gateway. One-click unsubscribe headers and HMAC-signed links, enforced before a message is ever handed to a provider.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC checked continuously. Domain health surfaces a misaligned record before your open rate does.

Security

Built for the tenant who audits you

Email systems hold credentials for every domain you send from. Panmail is designed on the assumption that someone will eventually copy the database.

Two-factor authentication

TOTP for every user, and administrators can enforce it across their tenant rather than hoping.

Scoped API keys

Keys are bound to one tenant and carry an explicit scope set — email:send, providers:read. A key grants exactly what it was issued for, and is revocable and expirable.

Keys are never recoverable

API keys are stored only as SHA-256 hashes. Panmail cannot show you a key again, because it does not have it.

Credentials encrypted at rest

SMTP, IMAP, POP3 and webhook secrets must be readable to be used, so they're sealed with AES-256-GCM and redacted from every API response.

Signed tracking links

Open and click URLs are HMAC-signed. Delivery events can't be forged, and the click endpoint can't be turned into an open redirect.

Verified inbound webhooks

SendGrid ECDSA, Mailgun HMAC or a shared secret — verified before a single event is recorded.

Brute force gets nowhere

Five failed logins locks the account for fifteen minutes, per account, automatically.

Least privilege by role

Super Admin, Admin, Editor and Viewer. A Viewer reads analytics and configuration and cannot change one byte of it.

FAQ

Questions worth asking first

Does Panmail replace my email provider?

No. Panmail sits in front of the providers you already use — SMTP, SendGrid, Mailgun, your own Postfix box — and gives your applications one interface to all of them. You keep your provider relationships and your sending reputation; Panmail manages what happens around them.

What happens when a provider goes down?

Nothing your callers can see. Mail is written to a durable outbox before any send is attempted, so an accepted message survives a provider outage, a restart, or a deploy. Panmail fails over to the next configured provider, and anything classified as a temporary failure is retried on your tenant's backoff pattern.

Can I run it on my own infrastructure?

Yes, and the self-hosted tier is free. Panmail ships as a single binary with the dashboard embedded, an unprivileged Docker image, and a worked Kubernetes StatefulSet — a StatefulSet rather than a Deployment because each replica needs its own local storage. It runs on PostgreSQL or SQLite and applies its own migrations.

How is multi-tenancy actually isolated?

Tenancy is structural, not a filter someone might forget. Each tenant carries its own providers, templates, suppression lists and retry patterns, event data is keyed by tenant prefix, and every RPC passes through an enforcement interceptor rather than trusting the caller's claim. Provider selection is driven by the sending domain, so one tenant cannot send as another's domain.

What can Panmail see of my provider credentials?

They're encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM and redacted from every API response, including your own. They have to be decryptable to be used — anyone who tells you otherwise is describing a password hash, not a credential store. API keys are different: those are stored only as SHA-256 hashes and cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.

Is there a real throughput number?

Panmail's own pipeline handles over a thousand messages per second with parallel workers and batched writes. Your ceiling, though, is almost always your provider's rate limit and connection concurrency — not the gateway. Benchmark against your own setup rather than trusting a headline figure, ours included.

Which databases are supported?

PostgreSQL and SQLite, both with automatic migrations. MySQL and MariaDB are not supported — the query layer uses PostgreSQL-style positional parameters, so those drivers connect and then fail on every query. We'd rather say so here than have you find out in staging.

Can I migrate off it later?

Your templates, suppression lists and delivery history export as open formats, and the self-hosted build is the same software as the managed one. Switching from cloud to your own hardware is a data import, not a rewrite.
